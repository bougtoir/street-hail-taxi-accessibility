# NOVELTY ASSESSMENT — STRESS-TEST FINAL

Novel (claimed): (1) explicit demonstration that large self-optimization
gains can be misleading in a public-data urban proxy, with quantified
transfer and null magnitudes; (2) cross-specification transfer used as a
validity stress test on an urban opportunity surface; (3) spatial
permutation nulls applied to relocation-optimization gains, showing the
magnitude is generic max-selection while prevalence is only marginally
non-random; (4) the integrated reusable audit framework.

Not novel (not claimed): pedestrian relocation, walking to catch taxis,
taxi recommendation (T-Finder uses trajectories), accessibility indices,
kernel surfaces, permutation testing in general.

No "first" claims used. Literature check: we are not aware of a prior urban
proxy study applying this exact triple audit to relocation optimization;
stated as a gap, not a verified first.
