2. **Final title:** "Stress-testing public-data urban accessibility
   proxies: street-hail taxi relocation without fleet telemetry".
3. **One-sentence contribution:** a reusable internal stress-test
   framework — component ablation, cross-specification transfer, and
   spatial permutation nulls — showing that large apparent gains from
   optimizing a public-data urban proxy can arise from surface
   heterogeneity rather than transferable spatial structure.
4. **Three strongest results:** (a) self-optimization produces large
   apparent gains (98.4% origins improved, +1.50 SD at 300 m) that are
   partly structural by construction; (b) cross-specification transfer
   between road-only and attractor-only is weak (53%/44% positive) while
   the combined optimum still encodes information beyond the
   nearest-major-road heuristic (82% origins, +0.22 SD, p<0.001);
   (c) permutation nulls reproduce improvement prevalence almost fully
   (p=0.02-0.09 advantage only) and produce LARGER gain magnitudes
   (2.61 vs 1.50 SD at 300 m).
5. **Three most important limitations:** no external ground truth (score
   uncalibrated to real vacant-taxi passage/waiting time); temporal
   structure partly assumed; single-city application with an unweighted
   station term (N02 FY2023 lacks passenger counts).
6. **Exact interpretations:** self-optimization = mechanical local-maximum
   selection, Level-1 evidence only; transfer = internal validity check,
   weak between distinct families; permutation null = quantifies generic
   maximum-selection, showing prevalence marginally non-random but
   magnitude fully generic.
7. **Why not "maxima exceed starting values":** the paper quantifies how
   much of the gain is structural vs transferable vs non-random —
   discrimination impossible from the trivial observation alone.
8. **Beyond taxis:** applies to any constructed urban surface optimized
   without ground truth (accessibility, amenity, safety, EV, micromobility
   proxies).
